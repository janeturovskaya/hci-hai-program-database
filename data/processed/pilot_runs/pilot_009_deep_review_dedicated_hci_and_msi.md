## Programs reviewed

### Indiana University — MS Human-Computer Interaction Design

- **Methods training:** Direct program evidence supports a two-year on-campus STEM HCI/d program with core courses in interaction design, design strategy, and user research. The curriculum emphasizes hands-on collaborative studios with real clients and flexible electives. School-level HCI/d context adds design-oriented methods such as co-design, speculative design, design criticism, alignment diagrams, and probe studies.
- **Human-AI / AI-product relevance:** Direct evidence is present but mostly elective/contextual. The program page says students can blend data science or AI backgrounds with HCI/d work and lists Human-Robot Interaction among past/current electives. School-level research context includes associated centers/labs such as R-House Human-Robot Interaction Lab, but this is indirect and does not prove MS placement.
- **Research or applied-project access:** Direct applied-project evidence is strong through required studios with real clients. Research access remains less certain: school-level research-area and lab context exists, but the reviewed official sources did not establish a thesis option, directed research pathway, or guaranteed lab participation for HCID master’s students.
- **Quant/data/technical development:** Technical/quant evidence is moderate and mostly optional. The program is STEM-designated; electives include HRI, advanced prototyping, Arduino prototyping, VR, and information architecture, and the page says no coding knowledge is required.
- **Career relevance:** Direct program evidence is strong. Indiana reports HCID alumni roles including UX Designer, UX Researcher, Product Manager, UX Engineer, Product Designer, User Experience Designer, Design Researcher, and Product Strategist, plus example employers including Google, Deloitte, SeatGeek, and ServiceNow.
- **Practical structure:** On-campus; fall start; described as a two-year STEM program. Admission language says there are no strict prerequisites or expectations about background beyond interest in human-centered design, with a bachelor's degree and minimum GPA expectation.
- **Key unresolved questions:** Exact required course list and studio sequence should be verified against the bulletin/program plan; direct master’s research, thesis, independent study, or lab-access mechanisms are still unknown; AI-product coursework appears elective rather than required.

### University of Maryland — MS Human-Computer Interaction

- **Methods training:** Direct program evidence is strong. UMD describes a 30-credit HCIM curriculum with core courses in programming, HCI, UX, UI, research methods, data analysis, and design. The capstone explicitly includes user research and prototyping for real-world contexts.
- **Human-AI / AI-product relevance:** Direct program-level elective evidence is meaningful. UMD lists elective areas including social computing, machine learning, Generative AI in UX, data visualization and design, usability testing, information architecture, and visual design. This is not required-course evidence for all students.
- **Research or applied-project access:** Direct evidence is strong for both professional and research pathways. All students complete either a two-semester client-based capstone or a two-semester thesis. The thesis is described as research-focused, defended, and supervised by a faculty member, making UMD the clearest of the four for an explicit master’s thesis path.
- **Quant/data/technical development:** Direct program evidence includes programming and data analysis in the core. Elective areas add data visualization, machine learning, and Generative AI in UX.
- **Career relevance:** Direct evidence is strong. UMD reports hands-on learning with partner organizations and industry professionals, access to Washington D.C. internships/jobs, and 94% employed within one year. Career relevance is strong for general UX/HCI; AI-specific outcomes remain less specific.
- **Practical structure:** 30 credits; STEM-certified; full- or part-time; intended two-year/four-semester completion; capstone or thesis in year two. Internships are valued but not required and do not receive college/course credit.
- **Key unresolved questions:** Which AI/ML/Generative AI electives are regularly offered and accessible to HCIM students needs confirmation; direct HCIL lab employment or research assistantship access was not established by the reviewed program pages.

### Drexel University — MS Human-Computer Interaction and User Experience

- **Methods training:** Direct program evidence is strong. Drexel says the MS-HCI/UX teaches quantitative and qualitative research methods, idea generation, prototyping, evaluation, accessibility, AI applications, foundations of HCI/UX, and empirical research methods.
- **Human-AI / AI-product relevance:** Direct program evidence is strong. Drexel explicitly frames the program as UX design and UX research for the AI age, names human-AI interaction coursework, and says students can select electives for a human-centered AI pathway including Applied Artificial Intelligence and Recommender Systems.
- **Research or applied-project access:** Applied-project access is strong: the program emphasizes hands-on projects and teamwork, culminates in a collaborative capstone, offers graduate co-op to eligible on-campus full-time students, and mentions research labs and partnerships with industry, healthcare, and cultural institutions. Direct thesis or guaranteed lab placement evidence was not found in the reviewed source.
- **Quant/data/technical development:** Evidence is moderate-to-strong through quantitative methods, empirical research methods, optional programming/computation skill-building, and elective options across information visualization, data science, application development, Applied AI, and Recommender Systems.
- **Career relevance:** Direct evidence is strong. Drexel says graduates complete the degree with a portfolio and lists employers including The Philadelphia Inquirer, Chatham Financial, Lowe’s, Pluto TV, Accenture, Morgan Stanley, UPMC, and Shopify. Student/co-op examples include UX research and end-to-end UX work.
- **Practical structure:** Full-time or part-time; 1.5 years full-time or 2.5 years part-time; on campus or online; fall/winter starts; no technical experience required; 45 quarter credits; capstone required; co-op available for on-campus full-time students.
- **Key unresolved questions:** Required course titles and exact distribution should be checked in the Drexel catalog; research-lab access for master’s students is described generally but not guaranteed; AI pathway details and availability need catalog-level confirmation.

### University of Michigan — MSI User Experience Research and Design pathway

- **Methods training:** Direct pathway evidence is strong. The UXRD pathway prepares students to define valid problems, uncover user needs, document UX research results, prototype solutions, and communicate with stakeholders. Required pathway courses include Introduction to Interaction Design, Information Architecture, and Needs Assessment and Usability Evaluation.
- **Human-AI / AI-product relevance:** Direct pathway evidence exists through listed options such as Gen AI and UX, Vibe Coding, AR/VR Application Design, IoT Interaction Design, Building Interactive Applications, and related technical/design courses. This is pathway-option evidence rather than a required Human-AI sequence.
- **Research or applied-project access:** Direct applied-project evidence is strong through SI 699 UX Research and Design, where students complete a semester-long project applying research and design methods. Program-wide MSI evidence also supports internship credit. A thesis option exists as an exception to the mastery course, but the reviewed official pages did not establish details for UXRD students.
- **Quant/data/technical development:** Direct evidence is strong for technical breadth: MSI core includes Programming I; UXRD method options include Intermediate Programming, Intro to Statistics, AR/VR, interactive applications, database application design, Gen AI and UX, and IoT interaction design. However, many technical/AI courses are options.
- **Career relevance:** MSI-level career evidence is solid but less UXRD-specific in the reviewed sources. Michigan says the MSI prepares information professionals to create data-driven solutions and purposeful technology and reports salary, employment, and job satisfaction outcomes. The UXRD page includes an alumni example landing a product designer role, but broader UXRD-specific outcomes need additional official evidence.
- **Practical structure:** 48 credits; majority finish in two years; part-time over three to four years is possible; students choose one of five pathways; required core, pathway prerequisites, selective requirements, electives, and SI 699 mastery course; up to nine internship credits can be approved.
- **Key unresolved questions:** UXRD-specific employment outcomes and employer/job-title lists need more targeted official evidence; thesis option details were not reviewed from an accessible official page; Gen AI and related course availability may vary by cohort.

## Comparative synthesis

- **Strongest for an experienced UX researcher with a psychology background:** UMD and Drexel currently look especially strong because both have explicit methods language covering research methods, data analysis or empirical methods, and practical UX research work. Michigan also has strong user-needs and usability-evaluation pathway evidence. Indiana is highly design-oriented and accepts varied backgrounds, but the exact required methods sequence needs more verification before promotion readiness.
- **Most promising for Human-AI / AI-product research:** Drexel has the clearest direct program-level Human-AI evidence because it explicitly names human-AI interaction coursework and a human-centered AI elective pathway. UMD is also promising through Generative AI in UX, machine learning, social computing, and thesis/capstone options. Michigan has relevant Gen AI and technical options. Indiana has HRI/AI-adjacent evidence, but it is more elective and ecosystem-oriented.
- **Strongest for applied professional development versus research access:** Drexel and Indiana are particularly applied-professional in the reviewed evidence, with studios/projects, capstone, portfolio, co-op or client work. UMD is strongest for explicit research access because students can choose a faculty-supervised thesis, while still supporting professional capstone work. Michigan is strong for applied project and internship-credit structure, but thesis/research access needs more detail.
- **Most unresolved uncertainty:** Indiana has the most unresolved uncertainty because required-course detail and direct master’s research access were not established from the reviewed pages. Michigan has moderate uncertainty around UXRD-specific career outcomes and thesis details. Drexel has uncertainty around catalog-level required courses and guaranteed lab access. UMD has the least structural uncertainty, though AI elective availability and direct lab access remain open.

## Recommendation

### Later promotion-readiness review

- **University of Maryland — MS Human-Computer Interaction.** Rationale: strongest direct evidence for required methods/data/programming core, explicit capstone-or-thesis structure, direct faculty-supervised thesis option, practical capstone, and solid career outcomes.
- **Drexel University — MS Human-Computer Interaction and User Experience.** Rationale: strongest direct Human-AI/HCI-UX program framing, clear qualitative/quantitative methods evidence, capstone/co-op/applied portfolio evidence, and explicit psychology/non-technical background fit.

### Keep as candidates pending more evidence

- **Indiana University — MS Human-Computer Interaction Design.** Rationale: strong dedicated HCI/d and career evidence, but promotion-readiness should wait for exact required-course sequence and direct research-access evidence.
- **University of Michigan — MSI User Experience Research and Design pathway.** Rationale: strong UXRD pathway and technical options, but promotion-readiness should wait for UXRD-specific career outcomes, more precise thesis/research access details, and confirmation of AI-course availability.
