# Pilot 011: All-candidate comparison and decision shortlist

Date: 2026-06-19.

This synthesis uses only evidence already present in the repository, especially the candidate intake, triage, deep-review, and evidence-collection notes from pilots 007–010. It does not add sources, change candidate records, change scores, promote programs, or create a numeric ranking.

The comparison is framed around Evgeniia’s goal: build on substantial existing UX research and psychology experience rather than repeat entry-level UX training; develop stronger Human-AI, Human-Centered AI, and AI-product evaluation capability; increase technical, quantitative, data, and AI literacy; retain meaningful qualitative and mixed-methods research strength; and keep a realistic path toward applied AI/UX research, product research, or research-adjacent roles.

Qualitative labels used below mean:

- **strong direct evidence** — repository evidence directly supports this dimension for the named program.
- **promising but elective/pathway-dependent** — repository evidence supports the dimension, but it appears to depend on electives, pathways, focus areas, or student choices.
- **indirect or limited evidence** — repository evidence is contextual, partial, historical, or not clearly tied to the specific master’s experience.
- **insufficient evidence** — current repository evidence does not support a confident claim.

## 2. Decision tiers

These tiers reflect current fit with Evgeniia’s goals and the amount of repository evidence available. They do **not** imply that lower-tier programs are weak overall, and they are not a numeric ranking.

### Immediate shortlist

Programs worth a later promotion-readiness and application-feasibility review because current repository evidence shows comparatively direct alignment with Evgeniia’s desired combination of advanced UX/methods, Human-AI or AI-product relevance, technical/data development, and applied or research structure.

- **University of Maryland — MS Human-Computer Interaction.** Strong direct methods, programming/data-analysis core, and unusually clear capstone-or-thesis structure make this a strong fit for someone who already has UXR and psychology experience and wants more technical/research structure.
- **Drexel University — MS Human-Computer Interaction and User Experience.** The repository has direct evidence for qualitative/quantitative methods, empirical research, AI-age UX framing, human-AI interaction coursework, and a human-centered AI pathway.
- **UC Berkeley — MIMS.** Evidence is broad across UX/HCI, AI/data/product careers, UX-for-AI coursework, HCI research context, and final-project structure, though the pathway appears elective-dependent.
- **UT Austin — MS Information Studies.** Evidence strongly supports HCI, UX research/design, Human-Centered AI and Data Science, Human-AI Interaction, ML, prompt engineering, data wrangling, and search/UX coursework; the main missing piece is the applied/research structure.
- **University of Michigan — MSI User Experience Research and Design pathway.** The UXRD pathway has strong methods and project evidence plus technical/AI options; remaining concerns are thesis/research detail, course availability, and UXRD-specific outcomes.

### Strategic alternatives

Strong possibilities that need one or two targeted checks before deciding whether they belong in the final application-readiness set.

- **Indiana University — MS Human-Computer Interaction Design.** Strong dedicated HCI/design and client-studio evidence, with some HRI/AI-adjacent evidence, but the required course sequence and direct master’s research access are not yet clear.
- **Cornell University — MPS Information Science.** Promising information-science/UX/data/interactive-technology fit with HCI and Human-AI department context, but current evidence is focus-area and context-heavy rather than a confirmed required HCI/HAI pathway.
- **UC Irvine — MHCID.** Strong dedicated HCI/design, methods, and capstone evidence; Human-AI/AI-product relevance is the main unresolved gap for Evgeniia’s specific goal.
- **Northwestern University — MS Artificial Intelligence.** Strong AI-product and Human-AI systems evidence, but the program is not a dedicated HCI/UX degree and needs targeted checks on UX research methods, HCI depth, and applied project structure.

### Contextual options

Useful comparisons, but currently less aligned with Evgeniia’s stated goal based on repository evidence. These may still be good programs for other goals or constraints.

- **DePaul University — MS HCI.** Strong dedicated HCI, methods, practicum/capstone, and online/in-person evidence, but AI/HAI relevance remains thin.
- **Arizona State University — Online MS User Experience.** Useful as an online UX option with direct UX methods and career-category evidence, but current evidence does not establish HAI, technical depth, capstone, or research intensity.
- **RIT — HCI MS.** Strong dedicated HCI identity and psychology/human-behavior framing, but current catalogue-level capstone/thesis, AI/HAI, lab access, and career details remain underdeveloped in repository evidence.
- **University of Chicago — MA Computational Social Science.** Strong computational social science, data, programming, mixed-methods, and thesis evidence, but HCI/HAI/AI-product alignment is indirect.

## 3. Recommended next 5

These five programs are recommended for a final, deeper application-readiness review. This is a qualitative shortlist for further review, not a ranking and not a final application recommendation.

### University of Maryland — MS Human-Computer Interaction

UMD appears to add structure beyond Evgeniia’s existing UX research and psychology background through a required core that includes programming, HCI, UX/UI, research methods, data analysis, and design. It also preserves research depth through a clear choice between a client-based capstone and a faculty-supervised thesis, while offering AI-adjacent electives such as machine learning and Generative AI in UX. **Most important question to verify:** are the AI/ML/Generative AI electives regularly offered and realistically accessible to HCIM students, and is there direct HCIL or lab access for master’s students?

### Drexel University — MS Human-Computer Interaction and User Experience

Drexel appears to add a direct Human-AI/AI-product layer beyond prior UXR experience because current evidence explicitly frames the program around UX research/design for the AI age and names human-AI interaction coursework. It also keeps qualitative and quantitative methods, empirical research methods, capstone, co-op/portfolio, and psychology/non-technical background fit in view. **Most important question to verify:** what is the current catalog-level required course distribution, and how reliably can students complete the human-centered AI pathway?

### UC Berkeley — MIMS

Berkeley appears to add breadth beyond existing UXR/psychology through an information-school program spanning UX/HCI, AI, NLP, data science, product management, data/ML/AI outcomes, and a final-project structure. It is especially relevant as a bridge between UX research, AI-product evaluation, and data/technical literacy, but much of the fit appears pathway- and elective-dependent. **Most important question to verify:** which HCI/AI/UX-for-AI courses are consistently available to MIMS students, and is there meaningful master’s research or lab access beyond the final project?

### UT Austin — MS Information Studies

UT Austin appears to add a strong Human-Centered AI and data-science dimension beyond entry-level UX training, with repository evidence for Human-AI Interaction, HCAI design methods, psychological/societal AI impacts, prompt engineering, machine learning, data wrangling, information retrieval/search, UX research/design/evaluation, and accessible UX. This combination is closely aligned with applied AI/UX research and AI-product evaluation if a coherent course plan can be built. **Most important question to verify:** can an MSIS student formally assemble a coherent HCI/UX/HCAI pathway with a capstone, thesis, practicum, internship, or research assistantship structure?

### University of Michigan — MSI User Experience Research and Design pathway

Michigan appears to add technical and product-facing breadth beyond Evgeniia’s existing UXR/psychology foundation through the MSI programming core, UXRD pathway requirements, SI 699 applied project, internship-credit structure, and options such as Gen AI and UX, AR/VR, IoT, interactive applications, databases, and statistics. It retains meaningful UX research depth through needs assessment, usability evaluation, user-needs work, prototyping, and stakeholder communication. **Most important question to verify:** how predictable are AI/technical course offerings for UXRD students, and what direct thesis, research, or lab opportunities exist for master’s students?

## 4. Gaps to resolve before choosing programs

These are unresolved questions for later review. This pilot does not research or answer them.

### Admissions/prerequisites

- Confirm whether each shortlisted program expects programming, statistics, portfolio, design, psychology, or professional UX experience at admission.
- Distinguish programs that genuinely build from non-technical psychology/UX backgrounds from those that may require technical preparation before entry.
- Check whether AI-oriented programs or pathways require stronger math, CS, programming, or data prerequisites than current repository notes show.

### Cost and funding

- Compare tuition, fees, living costs, online versus in-person cost differences, and expected program length.
- Identify scholarships, assistantships, fellowships, employer sponsorship compatibility, and restrictions for professional master’s students.
- Separate funding possibilities for research-oriented paths from general merit or need-based aid.

### Online/in-person practical fit

- Confirm which programs are in-person, online, hybrid, full-time, part-time, or cohort/lock-step in practice.
- Verify whether online students receive equivalent access to capstones, practicums, portfolios, career services, and faculty interaction.
- Check geographic constraints for internships, client projects, labs, and local AI/UX ecosystems.

### Master’s research access

- Verify whether master’s students can pursue thesis work, directed research, independent study, lab participation, RA roles, or faculty-supervised research projects.
- Distinguish guaranteed curriculum structures from competitive or informal lab access.
- For programs with capstones or final projects, clarify whether those projects can support AI-product evaluation, user studies, or Human-AI research questions.

### AI/HAI course availability

- Confirm which Human-AI, Human-Centered AI, AI-product, ML, data science, NLP, prompt engineering, recommender systems, or AI evaluation courses are required, elective, regularly offered, and open to the relevant master’s students.
- Distinguish course titles that sound relevant from courses that actually include human-subjects evaluation, UX research, sociotechnical AI, or product-facing AI work.
- Check whether AI/HAI pathways are formally supported or must be assembled ad hoc from electives.

### Career outcomes

- Find official outcomes that separate UX research, product research, design research, AI product, data/AI research-adjacent, HCI, and general technology roles where possible.
- Verify whether outcomes are program-specific or school-wide, and whether they apply to the exact degree/pathway under review.
- Look for evidence of internships, co-ops, client projects, alumni roles, or employers that plausibly support applied AI/UX research or research-adjacent career paths.
