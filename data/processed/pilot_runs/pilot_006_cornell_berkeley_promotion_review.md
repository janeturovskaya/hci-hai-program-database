# Pilot 006 — Cornell and Berkeley promotion-readiness review

Date: 2026-06-17

## Scope and constraints

This review covers only the two remaining candidate programs from the original five-program pilot:

1. Cornell University — Master of Professional Studies in Information Science
2. University of California, Berkeley — Master of Information Management and Systems

No new universities or unrelated programs were added. No automatic promotion was performed. The review uses official university, program, department, curriculum, course, research-area, faculty, and career/outcomes pages only.

## Promotion-readiness standard used

A `promote` recommendation requires strong official evidence that the program is meaningfully relevant to the project goal: U.S. graduate programs that are strong fits for a UX researcher with a psychology background seeking deeper HCI, Human-AI Interaction, Human-Centered AI, and AI-product evaluation expertise.

Because both remaining candidates are interdisciplinary information-school programs rather than dedicated HCI degrees, this review distinguishes between:

- strong adjacent fit for the database topic; and
- enough evidence to promote now without needing a policy decision about adjacent InfoSci degrees.

- `cand_src_cornell_qian_yang_hai_fit_2026_06_17` — faculty/research ecosystem evidence for Human-AI and HCI fit.

### Strengths

- Strong official evidence that Cornell Information Science has HCI, Human-AI Interaction, and faculty ecosystem relevance.
- The MPS program offers optional focus areas aligned with UX, data science, interactive technologies, and social/social-systems topics.
- The academic-planning page supports practical professional structure and a real-world project component.

### Concerns and evidence gaps

- The MPS is an Information Science professional degree, not a dedicated HCI, HCDE, or MS-HCI program.
- UX/HCI/data fit appears flexible or optional rather than a required program spine.
- Direct official evidence for MPS-specific research access, lab access, or directed research is still indirect.
- Career evidence is plausible but remains less specific than Berkeley MIMS career outcomes for UX research, AI, and product roles.

### Recommendation: `keep_needs_review`

Keep Cornell MPS Information Science as `needs_review` for now. The official evidence supports a plausible adjacent fit, but promotion should wait for either stronger MPS-specific career/research/curricular evidence or a documented policy that adjacent InfoSci professional programs with optional UX/data/HCI pathways should be promoted.

### Strengths

- Berkeley has strong official evidence for HCI, UX research/design, AI, product, data, and career relevance.
- MIMS has explicit career outcomes for UX researcher, interaction designer, product, AI, machine learning, data, and research roles.
- Course-level official evidence includes a graduate HCI research course and a UX-for-AI course.
- Research-area evidence supports a broad HCI/faculty ecosystem.

### Concerns and evidence gaps

- MIMS is still an information-management/school-of-information degree rather than a dedicated HCI degree.
- HCI, UX, and AI fit may depend on elective path choices rather than required curriculum for every student.
- Direct evidence of guaranteed MIMS student lab placement, thesis access, or faculty-supervised research access remains indirect.
- Promotion would create the first canonical adjacent InfoSci program, so promotion criteria should be documented before or alongside promotion.

### Recommendation: `defer`

Defer promotion in this PR. Berkeley MIMS has the stronger promotion case of the two candidates and may be promotable after the project explicitly documents how adjacent information-school programs should be handled. The evidence is strong for relevance, but a promotion now would set policy for non-dedicated-HCI programs without a recorded promotion criterion.

## Cross-program observations

- Cornell MPS has meaningful HCI/HAI ecosystem evidence, but the MPS-specific path remains more optional and less directly tied to UX research or AI-product evaluation outcomes.
- The project needs a clearer promotion policy for adjacent InfoSci programs before these candidates are converted into canonical programs.
