# Pilot 017: Deep review of newly added high-potential HAI candidates

Date accessed: 2026-06-19.

Scope: official-source evidence review of five newly added candidates from Pilot 016. This report does not add programs, change statuses, score candidates, update canonical/manual records, modify Pilot 015, or promote candidates.

## Syracuse University — MS Applied Human-Centered Artificial Intelligence

**Strongest direct evidence**

- Syracuse's official program page describes an AI master's focused on mastering AI technologies while remaining ethical and human-centered, with a hands-on application portfolio at the end of the program.
- The official catalog/program evidence identifies the degree as a 31-credit Applied Human Centered AI MS using advanced machine-learning packages and APIs with a human-centered approach.
- Graduate IST course evidence includes Human-Centered AI Applications, Responsible AI, Dynamics of Human-AI Interaction, Natural Language Processing, Deep Learning in Practice, Applied Machine Learning, Quantitative Reasoning for Data Science, and Applied Human Centered AI Portfolio.
- CAIRE Lab evidence shows an official critical-AI research environment with graduate students among current members and research themes in critical AI, platform/algorithm studies, justice informatics, and HCI-adjacent backgrounds.

**Required versus optional**

- Required: the program identity, overall HCAI orientation, and portfolio structure appear central to the degree.
- Optional/uncertain: exact required-versus-elective placement of specific HAI, responsible-AI, NLP, deep-learning, and methods courses needs final degree-plan verification from the catalog/advising pages. Lab participation is not guaranteed.

**Likely value beyond Evgeniia's current UXR and psychology background**

- Adds direct AI-product building exposure, ML/API literacy, generative-AI application work, responsible-AI framing, NLP/deep-learning options, and a portfolio artifact beyond traditional UX research.
- Best fit if she wants to reposition from advanced UXR toward AI-product researcher, human-AI interaction designer/researcher, or AI product evaluation roles with hands-on technical credibility.

**Main risks or gaps**

- The program is new, so alumni outcomes and stable course cadence are likely less proven.
- Evidence for rigorous qualitative/mixed-methods research training is less explicit than in mature HCI/UX programs.
- Research participation exists in the iSchool ecosystem, but the reviewed evidence does not guarantee MS placement in labs or assistantships.

**Recommendation: advance to application-readiness review**

Syracuse should move forward because it is one of the few reviewed programs with a degree-level HCAI identity, practical portfolio structure, responsible-AI/HAI course evidence, and technical AI coursework. The application-readiness review should verify the binding curriculum map, course availability, portfolio expectations, and realistic MS research/lab access.

## Stevens Institute of Technology — MS Human-Centered AI

**Strongest direct evidence**

- Stevens officially lists Human-Centered AI, M.S. under Computer Science.
- The official launch story says the Fall 2026 master's blends technical foundations of AI and machine learning with interdisciplinary expertise to make AI systems useful, accessible, and responsible.
- The same launch evidence frames the degree around design and psychology for understanding people's needs, perceptions, and behavior around AI systems.
- Stevens Computer Science research evidence includes AI/ML, data science, software systems, and HCI, and says students work alongside faculty on real-world challenges.
- The Stevens Institute for Artificial Intelligence AIRS Fellowship explicitly includes rising master's students in summer AI research with faculty mentors.

**Required versus optional**

- Required: program-level HCAI identity is clear, but catalog-level required courses were not found in this review.
- Optional/uncertain: research fellowship access is competitive/optional; exact methods, HAI, responsible-AI, ML, capstone, practicum, and thesis requirements remain unverified because the program is launch-stage.

**Likely value beyond Evgeniia's current UXR and psychology background**

- Potentially strong bridge from psychology/UXR into technical HAI because the official framing explicitly combines AI/ML with design and psychology.
- Stevens' AI institute and CS research ecosystem could provide applied AI and research exposure if the new curriculum is implemented as described.

**Main risks or gaps**

- Highest implementation risk among the five because the MS launches Fall 2026 and a full catalog page/curriculum was not found.
- No official evidence yet for required qualitative, usability, mixed-methods, or human-evaluation training.
- Career relevance is plausible but not yet backed by mature program outcomes.

**Recommendation: keep as strategic alternative**

Stevens is promising and highly on-theme, but it should not overtake mature shortlist programs until catalog requirements, applied project structure, and MS research access are confirmed. Keep it as a strategic alternative and revisit once the Fall 2026 curriculum is public.

## George Mason University — MS Applied Information Technology with Human-AI Interaction pathway

**Strongest direct evidence**

- George Mason's catalog shows an Applied Artificial Intelligence Technologies pathway with required foundations in applied AI, data processing, IT ethics/society, and AI application development.
- AIT 618 Human-AI Interaction covers designing, building, and evaluating AI-powered tools, HAI principles, front-end/back-end technical skills, design principles, and human factors.
- AIT 616 covers interactive machine learning, explainable AI toolkits, and human-facing tools for human-AI collaboration.
- HCI pathway evidence includes HCI, interaction design, research in applied IT, visualization/analytics, advanced HCI, Interaction Design and Accessibility, capstone, research project, and master's thesis options.
- Official college news shows active HCI student/faculty research presence at CHI, though not specifically limited to MS-AIT.

**Required versus optional**

- Required: the AAIT pathway appears to require applied AI foundations, data processing, ethics/society, and AI application development.
- Optional: Human-AI Interaction, Interactive ML/AI, capstone, research project, and thesis appear as electives in the relevant pathways. The HCI pathway makes HCI foundations stronger, while AAIT makes AI foundations stronger; a combined HAI plan would be advising-dependent.

**Likely value beyond Evgeniia's current UXR and psychology background**

- Strong technical growth: applied AI, data processing, AI application development, ML, NLP, interactive ML/XAI, interpretable ML, visualization, and systems-oriented prototyping.
- Strong fit for AI-product evaluation if she can combine AIT 618, AIT 616, HCI/design/accessibility, and an applied capstone/research project.

**Main risks or gaps**

- Human-AI coursework appears elective rather than required.
- The degree is applied IT rather than a dedicated HCI/UX or HCAI degree, so Evgeniia would need careful course selection to avoid a generic IT/AI path.
- Research access for master's students is indicated indirectly through student research news and project/thesis options, not as a guaranteed lab pathway.

**Recommendation: advance to application-readiness review**

GMU should move forward because it offers one of the clearest official combinations of applied AI foundations, explicit Human-AI Interaction, interactive ML/XAI, ethics, HCI, visualization, and capstone/research/thesis options. The next review should build a concrete course plan and verify whether an MS-AIT student can formally combine AAIT and HCI elements.

## University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign — MS Information Management

**Strongest direct evidence**

- MSIM requires 40 graduate credit hours and only three required courses: Data, Statistical Models, and Information; Sociotechnical Information Systems; and Information Modeling or Database Design and Prototyping, leaving substantial flexibility.
- The official course catalog describes IS 506 Human-Centered Information Systems as covering user needs, human-centered interfaces, interactive prototypes, unit testing, and user studies.
- MSIM pathways list Human-Centered Systems Design and Development with sample careers including UX Designer/Researcher, Usability Analyst, Product Manager/Designer, and courses such as Human-Centered Information Systems, Usability Engineering, and Advanced Topics in Human-Centered Design and Systems.
- Learning outcomes include human-centered design and systems, data analytics, research questions or real-world problems using appropriate scientific/research methods, communication to stakeholders, and ethical standpoints in sociotechnical design.
- Faculty/ecosystem evidence includes human-centered AI, human-AI teaming, responsible AI, social sensing, and big data analytics.
- FAQ evidence explicitly says MSIM students are not eligible for tuition-waiver teaching, research, or other graduate assistantships, but are eligible for graduate hourly positions, internships, and iSchool practicums.

**Required versus optional**

- Required: data/statistical modeling, sociotechnical information systems, and information modeling/database design.
- Optional: Human-Centered Systems Design and Development pathway, usability engineering, advanced HCD/systems topics, human-centered AI faculty engagement, internships, practicums, and graduate hourly roles.
- Explicit limitation: tuition-waiver assistantships are not available to MSIM students.

**Likely value beyond Evgeniia's current UXR and psychology background**

- Adds strong data/statistical modeling, sociotechnical systems, information modeling, human-centered systems, usability, and product/data-oriented pathways.
- Particularly useful if she wants an information-school route with flexible technical/data coursework and UX/product relevance rather than a narrowly branded HAI degree.

**Main risks or gaps**

- HAI/AI-product content is less degree-required and more elective/ecosystem-based than Syracuse, GMU, or Emory.
- Assistantship limitation is explicit and important for research-access expectations.
- Requires careful self-directed planning to avoid a general information management path that does not materially advance HAI/AI-product capability.

**Recommendation: keep as strategic alternative**

UIUC MSIM is a strong strategic alternative because it has excellent information-school flexibility, data/statistical foundations, sociotechnical design, usability, and practical options. It should not automatically enter the next application-readiness stage unless Evgeniia prioritizes UIUC's broader data/information ecosystem over direct HAI branding.

## Emory University — MS Computer Science

**Strongest direct evidence**

- Emory CS MS requirements state that all MS students must complete a practicum involving a substantial project or internship under an Emory faculty member or CS/Informatics industry expert.
- The Applied AI Practicum pairs teams of 3-4 pre-selected CS MS students with organizations to build AI/ML solutions, including code, models, prototypes, GPU-supported experiments, chatbots, analytics, and recommendation systems under faculty supervision.
- Emory's HCI research area explicitly includes cognition and visualization, dialog and recommender systems, information visualization and bias, AI/computing ethics, future of work, sustainability and IoT, broadening participation in computing, and Human AI Interaction.
- Graduate research/course ecosystem evidence includes machine learning, artificial intelligence, data mining, NLP, information retrieval, systems, visualization, HCI mentoring, conversational AI, LLMs, and recommender systems.

**Required versus optional**

- Required: CS MS practicum/project or internship is required for all MS students.
- Optional/uncertain: AI Practicum team participation is pre-selected; HCI/HAI research matching and specific electives depend on course availability, preparation, and faculty/project fit.

**Likely value beyond Evgeniia's current UXR and psychology background**

- Strongest pure technical upskilling path of the five: CS foundations, ML/AI, NLP, IR, recommender systems, visualization, systems, and applied AI project delivery.
- The required practicum and AI Practicum could create tangible AI-product artifacts that complement her UXR/psychology profile.

**Main risks or gaps**

- This is a CS degree, not a human-centered design or UX research degree; methods training may be less directly qualitative/mixed-methods unless she secures HCI coursework or research.
- Technical prerequisites and intensity could be a larger transition cost.
- HAI relevance depends on elective/project/lab selection rather than a required HCAI curriculum.

**Recommendation: advance to application-readiness review**

Emory should advance because it combines required applied project/internship structure with strong AI/ML/NLP/IR/recommender/visualization/HCI research evidence. It is especially relevant if Evgeniia wants a more technical AI-product research profile and is willing to manage the transition into a CS-heavy program.

## Impact on the current shortlist

Existing core shortlist for comparison: UMD HCIM, Drexel HCI/UX, UC Berkeley MIMS, UT Austin MSIS, Michigan MSI UXRD, and Iowa State HCI.

- **Syracuse MS Applied Human-Centered AI should enter the next application-readiness stage.** Compared with the existing shortlist, it adds a rare degree-level HCAI identity, responsible-AI/HAI coursework, technical AI/NLP/deep-learning exposure, and portfolio-based applied evidence. It may not be as mature as UMD, Drexel, Michigan, Iowa State, Berkeley, or UT Austin for classic HCI/UX methods, but it is unusually direct for AI-product capability.
- **George Mason MS Applied Information Technology should enter the next application-readiness stage.** It is less brand-aligned than the core HCI/information-school programs, but official catalog evidence is strong for applied AI foundations, Human-AI Interaction, interactive ML/XAI, ethics, HCI, visualization, capstone, research project, and thesis options. It deserves a next-stage course-plan feasibility check.
- **Emory MS Computer Science should enter the next application-readiness stage as a technical-growth option.** It is not a replacement for the strongest HCI/UX programs, but the required practicum plus Applied AI Practicum and HCI/HAI research ecosystem make it a credible AI-product research pathway if Evgeniia wants substantially more CS/AI depth.
- **UIUC MS Information Management should remain a strategic alternative.** It overlaps with Berkeley MIMS and UT Austin MSIS as an information-school option, but the evidence reviewed here makes HAI less required and research assistantship access more constrained than would be ideal. Its strongest value is flexible data/sociotechnical/HCD training, not a mandatory HAI pathway.
- **Stevens MS Human-Centered AI should remain a strategic alternative for now.** Its official framing is highly aligned with Evgeniia's HAI goal, but because it launches in Fall 2026 and catalog-level requirements were not found in this review, it should wait for implementation evidence before joining the application-readiness set.

Net effect: Syracuse, GMU, and Emory should be considered alongside the existing core shortlist in the next application-readiness review. They should not displace the current mature core programs yet; instead, they add three distinct hypotheses to test: dedicated HCAI portfolio path (Syracuse), applied IT/HAI/XAI path (GMU), and CS-heavy AI practicum path (Emory). Pilot 015 remains unchanged.
